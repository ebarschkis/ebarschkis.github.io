Erdős Problem #347

Prelude

So, I was bored (even though I should have been studying for my finals that would be next week) and I was again looking through the thousands of problems on erdosproblems.com. Neatly enough, there is a button on the top right which shows you a random problem in the database, so I went around until I stumbled over problem #347. To me the question sounded relatively simple compared to the others I saw. I (very very naively) thought, this problem was gonna be relatively easy to solve.

1.) What was the problem?

Paul Erdős asked whether there exists an increasing sequence of integers \(a_1\leq a_2\leq a_3\leq \cdots\) whose terms roughly double:

\[\lim \frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}=2\]

Now remove any finite collection of its terms. We can take any finite number of the suriving terms (even just a single one) and add them up, using each term at most once. Let \(S\) contain every positive integer we can make this way.

We want \(S\) to cover almost all the positive integers. To see what that means, count how many of the numbers \(1,\ldots,N\) belong to \(S\), then divide by \(N\). As we count further, that fraction should approach 1:

\[ \lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{|S\cap\{1,\ldots,N\}|}{N}=1. \]

The set \(S\) of sums may skip over infinitely many integers. That is allowed, as long as the missing numbers make up a fraction that tends to zero.

The difficulty is that the higher in the sequence you go, the sparser it gets. Every positive integer can be represented as a sum of distinct powers of two, which gives us the familiar binary representation. But remove even one of those powers, and we can no longer reach certain numbers with a sum of distinct terms.
